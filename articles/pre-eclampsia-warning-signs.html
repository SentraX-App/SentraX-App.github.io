Pre-eclampsia: Warning Signs Every Pregnant Woman and Family Should Know

By the Sentra-X Forte Limited editorial team3 min readUpdated 15 November 2026

Pre-eclampsia is a pregnancy condition in which blood pressure rises, usually after the 20th week, often with protein in the urine or signs that organs such as the liver or kidneys are affected. It can develop quietly and become dangerous for both mother and baby, which is why every antenatal visit includes a blood pressure check and a urine test. Knowing the warning signs, and acting fast, saves lives.

Who is at higher risk

  • A first pregnancy, or a long gap since the last one.
  • Pre-eclampsia in an earlier pregnancy, or in a mother or sister.
  • High blood pressure, diabetes or kidney disease before pregnancy.
  • Twins or more.
  • Age over 35, or being overweight.

Having a risk factor does not mean you will develop the condition, and it can also affect women with none.

Warning signs that need urgent care

Go to a hospital or clinic immediately if you have:

  • A severe headache that does not ease with rest.
  • Blurred vision, flashing lights, spots or loss of sight.
  • Pain in the upper right or upper middle part of the abdomen, just under the ribs.
  • Sudden swelling of the face or hands.
  • Shortness of breath.
  • Repeated vomiting.
  • The baby moving less than usual.

A fit or seizure is an emergency. Call for an ambulance or get to the nearest hospital without delay. While waiting, lay the woman on her side, keep her safe from injury, and do not put anything in her mouth. See our guide to home first aid basics and the first 60 seconds of an emergency.

Why antenatal visits matter

At each visit the health worker checks your blood pressure and urine, and sometimes your weight and swelling. These checks can spot pre-eclampsia before you feel unwell. Do not skip appointments, even when you feel fine. If you are given a home blood pressure monitor, use it as directed.

How it is treated

Treatment depends on how severe it is and how far along the pregnancy is. Mild cases are monitored closely with regular checks, and sometimes with medicine to lower blood pressure. Severe cases need hospital care, and doctors may give a medicine called magnesium sulfate to prevent seizures. The only cure is delivery of the baby and placenta, and the doctors will decide the safest time. Take any medicine you are prescribed, and do not start new tablets, herbal products or painkillers without asking.

After delivery

Pre-eclampsia can appear or continue in the days and weeks after birth, so keep watching for the same warning signs. Attend your postnatal check. Women who have had pre-eclampsia have a higher chance of high blood pressure and heart disease later in life, so ask for regular blood pressure checks in future. Read about the longer-term risks in high blood pressure complications.

Can it be prevented?

There is no sure way to prevent it, but good antenatal care helps. For women at high risk, a doctor may advise a low-dose medicine or calcium supplement during pregnancy. Do not start these yourself. Ask your health worker whether they are right for you.

How family can help

  • Know the warning signs, and take any of them seriously.
  • Plan transport to the hospital in advance, including who will call and who will drive.
  • Keep the antenatal card and contact numbers together and easy to find.
  • Go with her to appointments if you can.

When in doubt, go early. It is better to be checked and told all is well than to wait.

Key takeaways

  • Pre-eclampsia is high blood pressure in pregnancy, usually after 20 weeks.
  • Severe headache, vision changes, upper abdominal pain and sudden swelling need urgent care.
  • Attend every antenatal visit, even if you feel well.

Quick FAQ

Can pre-eclampsia happen after delivery?

Yes. Blood pressure problems can appear or continue in the weeks after birth, so keep your postnatal check.
