Recognising a Stroke Fast: The BE-FAST Checklist

By the Sentra-X Forte Limited editorial team2 min readUpdated 6 November 2026

A stroke happens when blood flow to part of the brain is blocked or a vessel bleeds, and every minute of delay means more brain cells lost. The BE-FAST checklist helps you spot it. Balance: sudden loss of balance or dizziness. Eyes: sudden blurred or lost vision in one or both eyes. Face: one side of the face droops when the person smiles. Arm: one arm drifts downward when both are raised. Speech: slurred or confused speech. Time: note when symptoms began and get help immediately.

Do not wait to see whether symptoms pass, and do not give food, drink or medicines by mouth because swallowing may be affected. Tell the emergency team exactly when the person was last seen well, as this determines which treatments are possible. High blood pressure is the biggest controllable risk factor for stroke, which is why consistent monitoring and taking prescribed medication matter so much.

Key takeaways

  • BE-FAST stands for Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time.
  • Note when symptoms began and call emergency services at once.
  • Do not give food, drink or tablets by mouth.

Quick FAQ

What if the symptoms go away on their own?

Brief stroke-like symptoms can still be a warning. Get medical help straight away anyway.
