How to Measure Your Blood Pressure at Home the Right Way

By the Sentra-X Forte Limited editorial team4 min readUpdated 15 November 2026

Home readings are only useful if they are taken consistently and correctly. A reading at home, in your own chair, away from clinic nerves, often shows what your blood pressure is really doing. A written log gives your doctor far more to work with than a single number from one visit. This guide explains how to choose a monitor, how to take a reliable reading, and what mistakes to avoid.

Choosing a monitor

  • Choose an upper-arm monitor. Wrist and finger devices are more easily thrown off by arm position and are generally less reliable.
  • Look for a validated device. Validated means it has been tested for accuracy. Ask your pharmacist or doctor which models they trust.
  • Get the right cuff size. A cuff that is too small can read falsely high, and one that is too big can read falsely low. Measure your upper arm and check the size range on the box.
  • Compare it once. Bring your monitor to a clinic visit and take a reading on both machines, so your doctor can see whether they agree.

Before you measure

Avoid caffeine, exercise and smoking for at least thirty minutes beforehand. Empty your bladder first, since a full bladder can raise a reading. Sit quietly for five minutes in a calm room.

How to take the reading

  1. Sit with your back supported, feet flat on the floor and legs uncrossed.
  2. Rest your arm on a table so the middle of the cuff is at heart level.
  3. Place the cuff on bare skin, not over a sleeve, with the lower edge about two finger widths above the elbow crease.
  4. Stay still and silent while the cuff inflates. Do not talk or scroll on your phone.
  5. Take two readings one minute apart and write down both numbers, with the date and time.

When and how often

Many doctors ask for readings morning and evening for about a week before an appointment, or on a schedule they set for you. Try to measure at the same times each day. Morning readings are best taken before breakfast and before your medicine, unless your doctor tells you otherwise. Follow your own doctor's plan, because it can differ depending on your condition.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring straight after a walk, a meal, tea or coffee.
  • Talking, laughing or watching something stressful during the reading.
  • Letting the arm hang down or rest unsupported, which can raise the number.
  • Crossing your legs or sitting without back support.
  • Taking one reading and panicking, or checking again and again until a number looks good.

Making sense of your numbers

Look at the average over several days rather than any single reading. Doctors often use slightly lower cut-offs for home readings than for clinic readings, and your own target may differ based on your age and other conditions, so ask what is right for you. Our article on blood pressure stages explains the usual ranges. If your readings stay at or above 130/80 across several days, share the log with your doctor rather than changing your medicine yourself.

When to seek help straight away

If a reading is above 180/120, rest for five minutes and measure again. If it is still that high and you have chest pain, breathlessness, severe headache, vision changes, weakness or confusion, get emergency help. If you feel well, contact your doctor the same day.

Looking after your monitor

Replace the batteries when the display dims, since low power can affect accuracy. Store the cuff flat and away from heat. Keep the log somewhere you will remember to bring it, and read our guide on how to read and share your blood pressure log for tips on presenting it to your doctor.

Key takeaways

  • Rest for five minutes, sit with your back supported and your arm at heart level.
  • Take two readings a minute apart, morning and evening, and write them down.
  • Use a validated upper-arm monitor with a cuff that fits.

Quick FAQ

Why are my home readings different from the clinic?

Some people are tense at the clinic and others relax at home. A written home log helps your doctor see the real pattern.
