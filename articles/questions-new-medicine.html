Questions to Ask Your Doctor or Pharmacist About a New Medicine

By the Sentra-X Forte Limited editorial team3 min readUpdated 15 November 2026

Many medication problems begin with questions that were never asked. A new prescription is a good moment to slow down and make sure you understand it. Doctors and pharmacists are used to questions, and clear answers help you take medicine safely and correctly. This article gives you a list to bring to your next visit.

What it is and why you need it

  • What is this medicine for, and what will it do?
  • How soon should I notice an effect, and how will we know it is working?
  • Is there a simpler or cheaper option, such as a generic version?

How to take it

  • Exactly when and how often should I take it, and for how long?
  • Should I take it with food or on an empty stomach, and with water?
  • What should I do if I miss a dose?
  • Can I crush, split or open it, or must I swallow it whole?

Side effects

  • What are the common side effects, and which ones usually settle in a week or two?
  • Which symptoms mean I should call the clinic, or stop and get urgent help?
  • Is it safe to drive or use machinery on it?

Other medicines, herbs and alcohol

Tell your doctor or pharmacist about everything else you take. This includes herbal remedies, supplements, vitamins and over-the-counter painkillers. Some combinations are harmful. For example, common painkillers such as ibuprofen can raise blood pressure and reduce the effect of some blood pressure tablets. Some herbal products interact with prescription drugs. Ask whether alcohol is safe with your medicine.

Special situations

Say if you are pregnant, planning a pregnancy or breastfeeding, because some medicines are unsafe. Mention any allergies and any past reactions to medicines. If you have kidney, liver or heart problems, say so.

Long-term use

  • How long am I expected to take it?
  • What happens if I stop suddenly?
  • What tests or check-ups will I need while taking it?
  • How and when do I get refills?

Never stop a long-term treatment simply because you feel well. For conditions such as high blood pressure and diabetes, feeling well often means the medicine is working. Our guide to taking medicine reliably explains how to build a routine.

Check the packaging

Buy medicine from a licensed pharmacy. Check that the pack is sealed, the expiry date has not passed, and that it carries a registration number from the national drug regulator, NAFDAC in Nigeria. If something looks wrong, ask the pharmacist. Do not share your medicine with others or use leftover tablets from an old illness.

Storage

Store tablets in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight, in their original packaging, and out of children's reach. Do not keep them in a hot car or bathroom.

Make it stick

  • Write down the answers, or ask the pharmacist to write the instructions on the pack.
  • Repeat the plan back in your own words to check you understood.
  • If you can, bring a family member who can help you remember.
  • Keep a written medication list and update it after every visit. Carry it with you.

If you are unsure later, call the pharmacy or clinic. Asking is always better than guessing.

Key takeaways

  • Ask what the medicine is for, how and when to take it and what to do if you miss a dose.
  • Tell your doctor about all supplements, herbs and painkillers you use.
  • Keep a written medication list and update it after each visit.

Quick FAQ

Can I take herbal products with my medicine?

Some herbal products interact with prescription drugs. Always tell your doctor or pharmacist before combining them.
